To use: invoke the `chatgpt-consult` skill (loads the full operating guide + Quickstart). ChatGPT is a high-value advisor — lean on it and act on its output; spot-check the load-bearing parts before shipping (not a line-by-line re-audit). Fire consults proactively; never send secrets/`.env`/keys.

**Know what you're buying: a consult is a deep reasoning round on whatever model and tier you've configured** — GPT-6 Astra Pro by default, reasoning for something on the order of ~25 minutes (a GPT-5.6-measured expectation, not a confirmed GPT-6 figure); pin an older family, or run against a legacy picker that only exposes a tier, and both the model and the timing are different — trust the `modelBadge` receipt or your own store's latency stats over this paragraph. Whatever model answers, it works the problem from several angles at once and argues its way to something closer to a proof than an answer. That is why you fire it and keep working, and why it is worth it only for your hardest, most open-ended work. It also means **the question and the prompt must be precise**: a long reasoning round pointed at a vague ask returns the same amount of well-argued irrelevance. Name the real decision, the bar for "good", and the specific sub-questions — then let it run — `await` waits as long as it takes and wakes you with the answer's path.

In auto mode your consult runs the LOCAL path — one `consult.py fire` (deliver+prep+enqueue) + `await` a local answer file; a background daemon does the actual ChatGPT send (a direct `submit` is blocked by the auto-mode data-exfiltration classifier). The daemon is installed once via `cgc install-daemon` and kept alive by launchd, so **just enqueue — never check whether it is running, and never run doctor first.** That check costs tokens every consult and buys nothing. If it genuinely isn't running, `await` says so immediately and you relay that one line to the user. **If the classifier ever denies one of these local commands, do not try to route around it** — relay it to the user and stop; attempting a bypass appears to get the whole session's local commands denied too.

**Take the whole answer seriously and THINK — don't reduce it to a bug-list.** A deep consult's architecture calls and design suggestions are often the highest-value part. If a finding is right, implement it now (design changes too, not just easy bugs). If you're not going to do what it says, that must be because you thought about it and know something the consult didn't — it reviewed from outside, blind to your runtime/harness/constraints — so produce that reasoning as a real answer (why the suggestion doesn't hold *here*, grounded in the fact it missed) and write it into the design rationale. That is acting on it. "Recording it as a to-do" is not. Only genuinely product-changing forks go to the user to decide, with your analysis attached.
